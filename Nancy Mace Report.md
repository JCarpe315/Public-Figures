## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Nancy Ruth Mace is a three-term U.S. Representative from South Carolina's 1st Congressional District whose political career has been defined by a paradox: she presents herself as an independent, grassroots outsider while operating within a dense network of corporate interests, dark money, defense contractors, and personal business entanglements that raise serious questions about whose interests she actually serves. This report documents a pattern of self-enrichment, conflicts of interest, and legal exposure that spans her pre-office business career, her congressional service, and her post-office transition planning.

**Key Findings:**

- **Real Estate Self-Enrichment**: Mace purchased a $3.9 million beach house during a heated 2022 primary campaign, financed with a $2.9 million mortgage, while simultaneously claiming financial hardship. She later sold her D.C. townhouse (co-owned with ex-fiancé Patrick Bryant) for $1.98 million to Dan Magy, CEO of Firestorm Labs—a defense drone startup backed by Lockheed Martin—while sitting on the Armed Services Committee. She then introduced legislation eliminating capital gains taxes on home sales that would directly benefit her own transactions. [HIGH]

- **Defense Contractor Entanglements**: As a member of the House Armed Services Committee, Mace secured $387.93 million in NDAA funding for South Carolina military installations while receiving campaign support from defense contractors (Booz Allen Hamilton, Boeing, Lockheed Martin via corporate PACs) and selling property to a defense tech CEO. [HIGH]

- **Bryant Corporate Network & Legal Nexus**: Her ex-fiancé Patrick Bryant operates a web of companies (Code/+/Trust, Go To Team, Assignment Desk, Harbor Entrepreneur Center) that intersect with federal contracting sectors Mace legislates over. Bryant is the subject of an active SLED criminal investigation (opened Dec 2023) for assault, harassment, and voyeurism. [HIGH]

- **Ethics & Financial Violations**: The Office of Congressional Conduct referred Mace to the House Ethics Committee (March 2026) for allegedly overcharging taxpayers $9,485.46 in improper D.C. lodging reimbursements while co-owning her D.C. residence with Bryant. She refused to cooperate with the investigation. [HIGH]

- **Dark Money & Outside Spending**: In the 2024 primary, outside groups spent $2.4 million against Mace, including $330,000 from the American Prosperity Alliance (McCarthy allies). She received $745,000 from Club for Growth in 2020. [HIGH]

- **Legislative Pattern**: Mace introduced 90 bills in the 119th Congress (5th most in the House), including legislation benefiting animal welfare groups that endorsed her, cannabis industry interests, federal contractors, and defense technology sectors aligned with her committee assignments and donor base. [HIGH]

- **Post-Office Planning**: After losing the 2026 gubernatorial primary (5th place, 12.1%), Mace announced plans to enter the private sector with interests in rare earth minerals, defense technology, and wellness—sectors directly adjacent to her committee work and donor network. [HIGH]

- **Unresolved Criminal Investigation**: The South Carolina Law Enforcement Division (SLED) has maintained an active criminal investigation into Bryant since December 2023, with multiple search warrants executed, but no charges filed as of the latest available information. [HIGH]

**Overall Assessment**: Nancy Mace represents a case study in the modern convergence of political office, personal business interests, and defense/corporate contracting networks. The evidence reveals a persistent pattern of using public office to advance private financial interests, surrounded by a network of associates under criminal investigation, while leveraging culture-war legislation to maintain a public profile that obscures the underlying financial flows.
