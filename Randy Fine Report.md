## How to Read This Report

This document is the product of an exhaustive open-source investigation. Every fact in here comes from a document we could find, verify, and tag. We are not guessing. We are not repeating other people's theories. We are laying out the evidence exactly as it appears, telling you where it came from, and letting you see the connections for yourself.

**A few terms you'll see throughout:**

- **Source Pedigree:** This tells you how solid the evidence is.
  - **HIGH** = Direct documentary evidence (FEC filings, court records, official transcripts, corporate registrations, patent records, ethics commission findings).
  - **MEDIUM** = Multiple independent news sources or corroborating accounts.
  - **LOW/UNVERIFIED** = Single-source reporting, often from a partisan outlet, that we could not independently confirm. We include these when they are widely cited but flag them clearly.

- **FEC:** The Federal Election Commission. This is the government body that tracks every dollar raised and spent in federal campaigns. Their filings are public record.

- **PAC:** Political Action Committee. A fundraising vehicle that pools donations to support candidates. Some PACs disclose their donors; others route money through layers that make the original source hard to trace.

- **AIPAC:** The American Israel Public Affairs Committee. One of the most powerful lobbying organizations in Washington, focused on U.S.-Israel relations. It operates both as a direct lobbying group and through affiliated PACs and a nonprofit educational arm called AIEF (American Israel Education Foundation).

- **AIEF:** American Israel Education Foundation. AIPAC's tax-exempt nonprofit arm that funds congressional trips to Israel. It has spent over $10 million on such trips in recent years.

- **SLAPP:** Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation. An anti-SLAPP law is designed to quickly dismiss lawsuits that are filed primarily to silence critics, rather than to address real legal harm.

- **DOAH:** Division of Administrative Hearings. In Florida, this is where ethics complaints against public officials go for formal adjudication.

- **SB 7014:** A Florida law enacted in January 2025 that weakened the state's ethics commission by restricting which complaints it could investigate to only those with "personal knowledge" or "information other than hearsay. Crucially, it applied retroactively.

- **TrackAIPAC:** An independent watchdog organization that tracks campaign contributions from pro-Israel lobbying groups to individual candidates.

- **Quiver Quant:** A financial data analysis platform that parses and visualizes congressional financial disclosures and FEC filings.

Think of this report as a very long detective's notebook. We are not claiming to have solved the case. We are showing you every clue we found, how the clues connect, and where the gaps remain.

### 1. The Ethics Commission Case (2022-Present)

**What Happened:** In April 2022, text messages obtained by Florida Today showed Fine telling West Melbourne City Councilman John Dittmore that state funding for the **Special Olympics** (which had received **$1 million** in state appropriations) and a **$460,000 flood risk reduction project** was "at risk" because the West Melbourne Police Department had invited school board member Jennifer Jenkins to a fundraiser and had not invited Fine. **The Protection:** Then, in January 2025, the Florida Legislature enacted **SB 7014**, which weakens the ethics commission by restricting it to complaints with "personal knowledge" or "information other than hearsay." **Fine's Response:** He called the Ethics Commission a "kangaroo court" and said it had a "personal axe to grind."
